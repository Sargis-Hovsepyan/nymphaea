      Nymphaea

      Your books

        Turn a manuscript into a book.

        Drop in a PDF, a Word file, an EPUB or plain text. Nymphaea sets it as an illustrated book you can read on a screen, in spreads, on paper or on an e-reader. Your words are never changed.

        You can also drop files anywhere on this page, or several episodes at once. Files stay on this device.

        What goes in

        • PDFPage numbers and running heads come out, split paragraphs are joined, italics are kept.
        • Word and EPUBHeadings, italics and line breaks become Markdown.
        • Web pagesOr paste straight from Word or Google Docs.
        • Markdown and textOne file, or a whole series, ordered by file name.

        How it becomes a book

        1. Check the textEverything becomes Markdown you can read and edit. Chapters, quotes and verse are found for you and listed, never changed silently.
        2. Place the picturesSmall drawings sit where the story changes scene, each composed from what its passage describes: the place, the hour, the weather, who is there. Each caption is a phrase quoted exactly from your words. A model on your own computer can paint them if you like.
        3. Choose a lookEight editions to start from, nine type pairings, six palettes, each in light and dark.

        What comes out

        • An HTML bookOne file. It works offline and can go straight onto GitHub Pages.
        • An EPUBFor Apple Books, Kobo and other e-readers.
        • A printed pageTrim sizes, mirrored margins, page numbers. Save it as a PDF.
        • MarkdownThe text itself, to keep or take elsewhere.

        Your words stay yours.

        Nymphaea only adds things around the text: pictures, spacing, type. A caption that isn’t an exact quote of your paragraph is replaced before it reaches the page, and every import is tested to keep every paragraph.

              Formatting help
              
        Start a line with # for a chapter or ## for a section. > makes a quote, and under a heading an epigraph. End a line with \ to keep its line break, for verse. *word* is italic, **word** bold, and * * * a scene break. Pasting from Word or Google Docs keeps italics and headings.
